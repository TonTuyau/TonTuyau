# TonTuyau 1.1.0

Follow-ups to [1.0.0](1.0.0.md): the answers of [QA15](../QA_SESSION_15.md) and [QA16](../QA_SESSION_16.md), clickable timecodes in descriptions, and the shorts player fixed in Firefox. Questions raised while building it: [QA_SESSION_17.md](../QA_SESSION_17.md).

## What's in it

- **No `data:` images in the Content-Security-Policy** (QA15-2 b): the policy now has `img-src 'self'`. Nothing used them; the build no longer inlines a small asset as a `data:` URL (`assetsInlineLimit: 0`), and `check.sh` (`check:csp`) now also fails on a `data:` URL in the built page or in its stylesheets.
- **Command-line sync progress: plain lines everywhere** (QA16-1), **with a heartbeat** (QA16-3 b). `tontuyau sync …` and `tontuyau index rebuild` print one plain line per update in a terminal too, as in the `tee` log: at each phase change, at most every 10 s, and each phase's last state before the next one. The in-place line (`\r`, clear to the end of the line, cut to the terminal's width) and the terminal detection are gone. During a long step with nothing new to report, the current line is printed again every 10 s, so the elapsed time keeps going up; the heartbeat never puts two lines of a phase closer than 10 s (with updates every few seconds there are no extra lines: the line due at each 10 s mark simply shows the latest state), and it stops before the report. Tests on a fake clock (`FakeTimeProvider`).
- **Clickable timecodes in descriptions.** On the watch page, a timecode in the description (`1:23`, `01:23`, `1:02:03`, `12:34:56`) is a link to that time of the video: a click seeks the player there and plays (YouTube's behaviour), and brings the player back into view if the page was scrolled away from it. It's a real link, `/watch?v=<id>&t=<seconds>`: a middle click or "open in a new tab" opens the video at that time, and the watch page now honours `t` when it loads (also YouTube's `83s`, `1m23s`, `1h2m3s`), so a time can be shared. `t` is front-only routing, no API change. YouTube's rules: seconds `00–59` on two digits, minutes `00–59` when there are hours; `3:5`, `1:60`, `25:00:00:00`, ratios such as `16:9`, numbers glued to letters or digits (`v1:23`, `1:23.5`) and times inside URLs stay text, and so do times past the video's duration (the API's `durationSeconds`). Rendered as text nodes and `<a>` elements, never `{@html}`. The shorts player shows no description, so nothing changes there.
- **Shorts in Firefox: the stall fixed** (one short wouldn't load and the app looked stuck, with no error). Reproduced in Firefox ESR 140 (headless, driven over WebDriver BiDi) through the production front server (`node server/main.ts`, HTTP/1.1) and the mock API, with real short files (AV1, VP9, H.264, 9:16 to 16:9) served at about 5.6 Mbit/s like a remote server, scrolling one short a second.
  - **The cause** (lead a, in another form than expected): the shorts player only ever has **one** `<video>` in the page, but a `<video>` taken out of the page **keeps downloading, and keeps its connection, until the garbage collector frees it**. Each short left behind went on fetching its whole file: up to **9 media downloads open at once for 1 video on screen**, every short downloaded entirely (313.6 MB for 35 shorts, not one request cancelled), and the next short's file waited behind them: a short stuck at "nothing loaded" for 8 s and more, no error anywhere. **Fix**: the player empties its `<video>` when it lets go of it (another short, another page, a message in its place): `pause()`, `src` removed, `load()`, which cancels the download at once.
  - **The wheel** (lead b, confirmed): Firefox reports a mouse notch as 3 *lines* (`deltaMode` 1), counted as 48 px against a 50 px step, so **one notch in two did nothing**; and a trackpad swipe of about 1 s (smooth scrolling, momentum) **moved two shorts**, the 600 ms pause ending mid-gesture. Now a line counts 40 px (one notch is a step, as in Chromium) and the wheel moves **one short per gesture**: a gesture is a run of wheel events with no gap over 250 ms; it moves once it has gone 50 px, then the rest of it is ignored, and a partial scroll no longer adds up with the next one (`src/lib/wheelGesture.ts`).
  - **Codecs** (lead c, ruled out): an undecodable file (random bytes under an H.264 type) shows "The browser couldn't play this file" within about 0.1 s in Firefox, before and after; this Firefox plays HEVC too. Hardening: the `HEAD` the player sends after a playback error (missing file or not?) now gives up after 10 s, so a request that never gets an answer can't leave the player waiting with no message.
  - **Memory** (lead d): no leaked listener or effect found; what grew was the left-behind downloads' buffers. Firefox's memory (all its processes) over 40 shorts: before 1.37 → 2.25 GB at its peak, after 1.20–1.44 GB, flat.

  | Firefox, shorts one per second | before | after |
  |---|---|---|
  | `<video>` elements in the page | 1 | 1 |
  | media downloads open at once (peak) | 9 | 1 |
  | TCP connections to the front (peak) | 9 | 2–3 |
  | data fetched for 35 shorts | 313.6 MB | 25.4 MB |
  | left shorts' downloads cancelled | 0 of 35 | 35 of 35 |
  | short ready to play: median / worst | 411 ms / 8,032 ms (stuck) | 209 ms / 431 ms |
  | stalls (not playable after 8 s) | 1 in 35 | 0 in 35 (and 0 in 40 on the long run) |
  | one Firefox wheel notch (3 lines) | moved 1 time in 2 | moves every time |
  | a 1 s trackpad swipe | moved 2 shorts | moves 1 |

  Tests: 12 shorts visited one after the other keep one `<video>` alive and leave each previous one emptied (no `src`, `load()` called); the player empties its video when it goes away and when a message replaces it; the bounded `HEAD`; the wheel gestures (lines, smooth scrolling, momentum, a spun wheel, partial scrolls).
