# TonTuyau 1.5.0

In progress on `release/1.5.0`; tried on the VM as `1.5.0-alpha.2`.

The first step of replacing TubeArchivist: TonTuyau reads YouTube itself. Metadata only: nothing is downloaded in 1.5.

## TonTuyau's own word on YouTube

- **Each video's and channel's state on YouTube.** TonTuyau keeps, in PostgreSQL, what it knows of each video and channel: not checked yet, on YouTube, to retry later (the last check had no answer to trust), or gone (YouTube says removed, private or terminated), when it was checked and what YouTube said. TubeArchivist's "unavailable" alone is "retry later", never "gone": TubeArchivist sets it when its own refresh fails, whatever the reason. So the **"Unavailable on YouTube" badge** now shows only what TonTuyau itself found gone, and the badges that came from TubeArchivist's flag go away until TonTuyau checks (contract v1.9: same shape, `active` is TonTuyau's word). The dashboard's "gone from YouTube" counts follow.
- **Refreshing a video, a channel, or a channel and its videos.** Three new tasks read them on YouTube (`POST /api/admin/tasks/{task}/runs?target=<id>` with `refreshVideo`, `refreshChannel`, `refreshChannelVideos`, contract v1.10; `tontuyau youtube video|channel|channel-videos <id>`). Each is a run like the syncs: one at a time with them, in the Tasks page's history with its log, cancellable. What YouTube answers:
  - found: its title, description, tags, categories, views, likes, comment count and channel name become TonTuyau's, and the TubeArchivist sync no longer changes them (TubeArchivist still brings the file);
  - removed, private or terminated: gone (the badge);
  - no answer to trust (a bot check, too many requests, the network): to retry; what TonTuyau confirmed before (on YouTube, or gone) stays.
- **Chapters**, always read with a video: their start, end and title.
- **Comments**, as the YouTube page's setting says: **none** (the default: refreshes stay quick), **the top ones** up to a number chosen (500 at first, YouTube's order), or **all**. Answers included, with their author, date, likes, and whether pinned, hearted or by the channel itself; each refresh replaces those read before, and when yt-dlp can't read them, those read before stay. Reading comments is slow: YouTube gives about 20 a request, so a video with 50,000 comments takes half an hour or more, and a big channel with its videos can take hours (the run can be canceled; so many requests may make YouTube ask for a bot check).
- **YouTube's pictures.** A refresh downloads the video's largest thumbnail, the channel's avatar and banner (uncropped), from YouTube's picture hosts only (`i.ytimg.com`, `yt3.googleusercontent.com`, `yt3.ggpht.com`; HTTPS, 10 MB at most), into TonTuyau's art volume in the place of TubeArchivist's copies, with their resized versions; the format (JPEG, PNG, WebP) is read from the picture itself. A picture that can't be had changes nothing (the run counts it). The TubeArchivist sync no longer replaces them.
- **Kept across index rebuilds.** All of it lives in PostgreSQL (`youtube_videos`, `youtube_channels`, `video_comments`): the sync lays it over what TubeArchivist says at every write, an index rebuild too, so nothing is lost when the index is rebuilt.

## The watch page

- **Chapters** in the video's description box, always shown (even folded): a row of cards, the one playing outlined, a click goes there.
- **Comments**, for a video TonTuyau read them for: how many YouTube counts, how many are kept and when they were read, in YouTube's order or the newest first, 20 threads at a time; answers open under their comment, long ones fold, timecodes are links to their time; pinned, hearted and the channel's own are marked. Authors get a letter, not their picture (the browser would fetch it from Google). Read and none: "No comments under this video"; never read: nothing. API: `GET /api/videos/{id}/comments`, `chapters` in the video (contract v1.13).

## The admin area

- **The Tasks page** has two groups: TubeArchivist (the syncs, as before) and YouTube, a card per refresh: paste a video's link or id (`watch?v=`, `youtu.be/`, `/shorts/`, `/live/`), or a channel's id or its `/channel/` link (an `@handle` can't be read yet), and it starts, then opens its run. A warning says when there's no YouTube cookie. The run history shows the refreshes, each under its own task, with what they read, found, found gone and left to retry (a channel refreshed with its videos counts its videos; the channel's own answer is a note of the run).
- **A channel's admin page** (`/admin/channels/<id>`, from the dashboard's new "Manage" link; the dashboard also counts each channel's videos to retry and not checked yet): the channel as TonTuyau knows it on YouTube (its state, when checked, subscribers, its videos, shorts and streams), how many of its videos are in each state, and its videos, latest first, by state or by words, each with its state, when it was checked and what YouTube said. From there: refresh the channel, the channel and its videos, or one video; the page follows the run and shows what changed. API: `GET /api/admin/channels/{id}`, `GET /api/admin/channels/{id}/videos` (contract v1.12).
- **The YouTube page** (`/admin/youtube`): the YouTube cookie (works, refused or not checked, when and from whom it came, what YouTube said; check it again, remove it), the tools' versions, which yt-dlp runs, and which comments a refresh reads (with who changed it and when).
- **The API keys page** (`/admin/api-keys`): making a key (its name and kind; its secret shown once, with Copy), and the keys made, latest first: their kind, the secret's first characters, enabled or not, when and by whom made, their last use; disabling, enabling again, deleting (asked first).

## Underneath

- **yt-dlp and Deno in the backend image**, pinned and checked against the checksums their releases publish ([yt-dlp](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp) 2026.08.19, [Deno](https://deno.com) 2.9.7, through [Ytdlp.NET](https://github.com/manusoft/Ytdlp.NET)). The image is about 160 MB bigger.
- **Which yt-dlp runs.** YouTube changes often, and yt-dlp follows: by default TonTuyau runs the **latest official release**, checked on GitHub at startup and once a day, and downloaded when there's a newer one. An admin can choose instead one official release (any with a Linux program: 2022.06.22 and later) or one **nightly build** of the last 2 months (`GET`/`PUT /api/admin/youtube/ytdlp`, `GET /api/admin/youtube/ytdlp/versions`). A version is downloaded only from yt-dlp's own GitHub repositories, checked against its release's checksums and tried before it's used, and switched to between tasks only. The image's copy is the start, and the fallback when GitHub can't be reached. The versions downloaded are kept in a new volume, `tontuyau-tools` (`/data/tools`).
- **The YouTube cookie.** TonTuyau reads YouTube signed in with the YouTube cookie of a browser where you're signed in (`PUT /api/extension/youtube-cookie`, with a browser extension key or an admin's session): it keeps only the `youtube.com` cookies, in PostgreSQL, checks them at once by reading the account's Liked videos (as TubeArchivist does), and gives them to yt-dlp as a file of its own, readable by the backend only, deleted after each run. Never shown nor logged. TonTuyau's browser extension, **LaPompe** ("LaPompe remplit TonTuyau"), comes after 1.5, in its own repository; without a cookie, refreshes read YouTube signed out (YouTube may then ask for a bot check more often).
- **API keys**, for programs: they send `Authorization: Bearer tt_…`. A key has a **kind** and opens only its kind's endpoints, never what a logged-in admin does; 1.5 has one kind, **browser extension**. As many keys as wanted, each with its own name (unique, whatever the case), shared by every admin; TonTuyau keeps only the secret's hash (SHA-256), its first characters, who made it and when, and its last use (contract v1.8).
- **The Reservoir.** The Elasticsearch service and container are now called `tontuyau-reservoir` (they were `tontuyau-es`): where all the tuyaux lead. Its data volume keeps its name.

## Upgrading

The Elasticsearch service has a new name, so this upgrade is done in this order:

1. Take the new `docker-compose.yml` (it also adds the `tontuyau-tools` volume). If you have a `docker-compose.override.yml` that names `tontuyau-es`, rename that key to `tontuyau-reservoir` there too (else Compose sees a service without an image).
2. Stop the stack, the old `tontuyau-es` container included (two Elasticsearch on the same data would refuse to start): `docker compose down --remove-orphans`
3. Pull and start: `docker compose pull && docker compose up -d`

No other configuration change. The backend adds its new tables (`api_keys`, `youtube_cookie`, `ytdlp_settings`, `youtube_videos`, `youtube_channels`, `video_comments`, `youtube_settings`) to PostgreSQL by itself at startup. The index gained the YouTube state, so the first start rebuilds it by itself, once, from TubeArchivist (a full import, as when the index is new: the library stays browsable meanwhile). The backend now reaches GitHub (`api.github.com`, `github.com`) at startup and once a day, for yt-dlp's releases, and YouTube and its picture hosts during refreshes.
